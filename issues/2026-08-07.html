First Black Woman Confirmed as CDC Director Amid Health Crises

HEALTH EQUITY — The U.S. Senate confirmed Dr. Erica Schwartz as the first Black woman to head the CDC in a 51-44 vote, making her the agency's 22nd director. The CDC is responding to the nation's highest measles case count in 35 years alongside a growing cyclosporiasis outbreak linked to shredded lettuce. Schwartz inherits a CDC reshaped by political battles over science and vaccines.

The takeaway
Black women leaders often face heightened scrutiny when appointed to crisis positions—Schwartz must navigate both institutional skepticism and public health emergencies.
Sources [Washington Informer] [TheGrio]
[ P2 · POLICY & JUSTICE · BRIEFING ]

National Guard D.C. Deployment Extended Through 2029 at $1.4 Billion Cost

POLICY & JUSTICE — The National Guard is continuing its deployment in Washington, D.C., through January 2029 at a cost of $1.4 billion, according to the Pentagon. The deployment has been criticized by local officials as an infringement of their city's autonomy and by Democrats who say it has militarized the streets at public expense. There are now some 4,600 troops in the area.

The takeaway
Billions in federal spending on military presence in D.C. raise questions about resource allocation when Black communities face cuts to social services and education.
Sources [Democracy Now!] [PBS News]
[ P2 · POLICY & JUSTICE · BRIEFING ]

SNAP Cuts Disproportionately Hit Black Americans as Work Mandates Tighten

POLICY & JUSTICE — The One Big, Beautiful Bill Act, signed into law in 2025, slashed $187 billion from SNAP over the next decade, triggering new work requirements and restricting what recipients can buy. More than 1 million adults aged 55 and older will lose food assistance under expanded rules, with new cuts and work requirements expected to affect Black communities most. More than 4 million people have already lost SNAP since the law took effect.

The takeaway
Black families making up 26% of SNAP recipients face the steepest consequences as federal food assistance shrinks alongside stricter barriers to eligibility.
Sources [AFRO] [Word In Black]
