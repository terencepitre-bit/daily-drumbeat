Predatory Auto Lending Targets Black Borrowers

ECONOMY & WORK — A Center for Responsible Lending report finds that predatory loan terms and dealer tactics drive delinquency and default for Black borrowers, who face high-pressure sales tactics, misleading claims, and aggressive upselling that obscure true vehicle costs. Hidden fees, interest rate markups, and remote vehicle disablement traps Black consumers in cycles of auto loan debt. Black borrowers disproportionately pay the highest interest rate markup, resulting in more than $3,000 in additional interest over the life of the loan.

The takeaway
Federal guardrails must be established to protect consumers, as was done in the mortgage market.
Sources [Spokesman-Recorder] [Washington Informer]
[ P5 · HBCUS & EDUCATION · BRIEFING ]

Houston Teens Retrace Civil Rights History on Freedom Tour

HBCUS & EDUCATION — Houston-area teenagers board a bus each summer to retrace the Civil Rights Movement's roads, visiting Selma, Tuskegee, Montgomery, Birmingham, Memphis, and New Orleans through the Freedom Tour, an immersive journey organized by the Expose Excellence Youth Program. Since its founding in 2021, Expose Excellence has reached more than 2,000 students through mentoring, after-school programs, leadership academies, cultural activities, and career exposure.

The takeaway
In a time when voting rights and historical truth remain national flashpoints, sending Houston students to Civil Rights landmarks is more than a field trip—it is a democracy investment.
Sources [Houston Defender] [Houston Style Magazine]
[ P11 · BLACK EXCELLENCE · BRIEFING ]

Chef Chris Williams Invests in Black Art and Culinary Legacy

BLACK EXCELLENCE — James Beard-nominated restaurateur Chef Chris Williams is behind new culinary and cultural concepts at the Historic Eldorado Ballroom, one of America's oldest Black music venues, marking his first Houston concepts outside his acclaimed restaurant Lucille's. A sister concept called Hogan Brown Gallery will highlight African American art and artists. Williams opened Lucille's in 2012 as a tribute to his great grandmother, Lucille B. Smith, considered Texas' first African American businesswoman.

The takeaway
Williams is on a mission to empower the Black community through entrepreneurialism while fighting agricultural oppression and uplifting African American foodways.
Sources [Forbes] [Resy]
