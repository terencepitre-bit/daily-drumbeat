El-Sayed's Michigan Win Signals Shift in Democratic Primary Dynamics

POLICY & JUSTICE — Progressive Abdul El-Sayed defeated Rep. Haley Stevens in Michigan's marquee Democratic primary for US Senate in a triumph for national progressive leaders. While many observers framed the race around whether El-Sayed could break through with Black Michiganders—who account for about 20% of the state's Democratic primary electorate—the primary bucked simple narratives about the state's electorate and set the stage for a high-stakes general election focused on how Black Michiganders voted, whether Democrats can unite their base, and what lies ahead in November. Senate control runs through the swing state, so reconciling tensions within their diverse coalition and winning the seat is vital if Democrats hope to secure a majority in the upper chamber.

The takeaway
Black voters' complex decision-making in Michigan revealed that Democratic primary races can't be reduced to simple progressive-vs-establishment narratives.

Faith Leaders Launch Intensive Push for Midterm Voter Mobilization

POLICY & JUSTICE — Less than 100 days out from midterm elections, faith leaders across the nation are hitting the pavement with their own campaigns to mobilize ahead of what many consider far more than a political vote. Rev. Dr. William J. Barber, founder of Repairers of the Breach and co-chair of the Poor People's Campaign, is calling for state-focused action, with emphasis on "vision-forward" voting—emulating 1964 when "they marched to the polls"—while the bishop hailed a call for 100 days of action planning nationwide calling on churches, synagogues, college campus groups, and street corners to mobilize with 1,000 marches to the polls. The fall election season is unfolding with various approaches to chart the path to the ballot box this November, coinciding with 61 years of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 on Aug. 6.

The takeaway
Faith communities are mobilizing record-scale voter campaigns that frame voting rights as a moral and spiritual imperative heading into 2026.
Sources [Washington Informer] [Interfaith America]
