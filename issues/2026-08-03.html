Pope Leo Addresses Black Ancestry and Slavery, Breaking Long Silence

BLACK EXCELLENCE — In his clearest public comments yet, the first American-born pope acknowledged the complicated family history that spurred discussions about race when he said his mother's family included both enslaved people and slaveholders. Leo has already demonstrated a willingness to confront slavery's legacy inside the Catholic Church, having earlier this year issued the Vatican's most direct apology for its historic role in legitimizing slavery.

The takeaway
The pope's personal reckoning with his ancestors' dual roles in slavery mirrors and adds weight to the church's institutional accountability for complicity in racial oppression.
