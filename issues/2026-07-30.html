[ P2 · POLICY & JUSTICE · BRIEFING ]

Police Accountability Demands Grow After Missouri Man's Custody Death

POLICY & JUSTICE — Lloyd Gilmore, a 44-year-old Sikeston resident, died in police custody after an altercation with Sikeston police officers on June 10. Police shocked him with a Taser and put him into hobble restraints, which allow officers to hogtie a person's feet and hands, according to a police report. Two autopsies were performed, but they — and Gilmore's cause of death — had not been released as of mid-July. The case has reignited calls for meaningful police reform and greater accountability measures across the nation.

The takeaway
Despite worldwide protests in 2020 in the wake of high-profile police-involved killings of Black people, the public paid little attention when a prestigious group released a report last year about deaths in custody.

[ P3 · ECONOMY & WORK · BRIEFING ]

Data Center Boom Drives Up Electricity Costs for Black Families

ECONOMY & WORK — The amount of power needed for data centers is expected to double by 2027 and quadruple by 2035. Despite President Trump's promise to slash electricity prices by 50% by July 2026, residential electricity rates have actually increased by 18%, disproportionately affecting Black households, with more than 1 in 10 Black households carrying overdue balances, three times the rate for white households. In the first half of 2026 alone, companies requested a combined $18.6 billion in rate increases.

The takeaway
Low-income residents, renters, Black households, and Hispanic households spend up to 20% of their income on energy, compared to 3% for higher-income households.
Sources [Capital B] [EESI]
[ P2 · POLICY & JUSTICE · BRIEFING ]

Education Dept. Eliminates Disparate Impact Protections in Schools

POLICY & JUSTICE — The Education Department will no longer consider school policies that disproportionately harm one group of students as discriminatory unless the discrimination is intentional, rescinding a decades-long standard for civil rights enforcement. Across the country, data shows Black students have been more likely to receive punishments that remove them from the classroom, such as suspensions, expulsions and transfers to alternative schools. Advocates said that the change would further diminish the Education Department's role in protecting students of color, in addition to the downsizing and transfer of the Office for Civil Rights.

The takeaway
The schoolhouse doors in 2026 don't have signs on it that say, 'whites only,' but discrimination is still very prevalent.
Sources [AFRO News] [Education Week]
